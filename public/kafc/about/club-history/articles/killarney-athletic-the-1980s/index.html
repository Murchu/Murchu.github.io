Killarney Athletic: The 1980s

Killarney Athletic Archive·2015 5 min read

The early ‘80s in the club saw Billy Doyle back to the ‘helm’ of ‘A’ team management while younger brother Frank played a more active role in the administration side of the club. Toni Fleming took over the ‘chair’ while Kevin ‘Plug’ Malley and Tom Ashe continued in their respective administrative roles that they held from the ‘70s. Antonio Cirillo who joined the club in the late ‘70s proved to be a very dedicated member of the committee. The organising and running of the 7-a-side had become the major focus of the club for three months of the summer as the tournament numbers swelled. The coverage and support of the local media contributed a lot to its increasing popularity.

The ‘Men from the Bower’ join Athletic

Another spin off from the 7-a-side was that there were many players getting the appetite to join the Kerry league clubs. Athletic gained with some members joining from the former ‘The Bower Rovers’ team (Rathmore). They were: Sean Moynihan, Dan Leary, Neily Kerins and the Mulcahy brothers of Donie and DD. Neily also managed teams at schoolboy level for the club and with Sean O’Sullivan had the privilege of managing Brendan Moloney of Nottingham Forest fame. DD Mulcahy was an excellent goal scorer and an outstanding club secretary in the ‘80s.

Limerick United play in the Half Moon in 1982

Frank Doyle was very keen to raise the profile of the club by bringing some League of Ireland teams to Killarney. To his credit he succeeded in landing Limerick United, the 1981/’82 FAI Cup winners to play a friendly against an Athletic selection in the Half Moon in September ‘82. The Limerick side who had qualified for European competitions for the 3rd successive year had many big soccer names in the club that season with Mia Dennehy, a former international and English League professional, the best known player while former Irish international manager Eoin Hand was also associated with the club. Needless to say Athletic didn’t win the match but scored on 3 occasions.

Cobh Ramblers v Killarney Athletic in 1983

Frank repeated his success in ‘83, when he attracts Cobh Ramblers the 1983 FAI Cup winners to town that September. This game was played on the St Finan’s Pitch that was to become the home ground for Celtic for many years. Bill Davidson a Cobh native managed Athletic on the night and for the early part of that season. Athletic were narrowly beaten by Cobh on a night when the club chairman Tom Griffin lined out in the back four. Bill was also to make an invaluable contribution with Connie O’Sullivan to the club’s underage teams over the following years.

The Dual Players

Some of the club’s better teams in the early ‘80s included a number of very good dual players, James Cronin, Connie ‘Doc’ O’ Doherty, Seanie and Pat O’Shea in particular were equally talented players in both codes. Connie also played for the KDL in the Oscar Traynor Cup, while Pat was a ‘wizard’ with the ball at his feet. Pat also had success as the Kerry team boss that won the 2007 All-Ireland Championship. Two other talented players of that era were John Casey and Pat Fennell.

‘B’ Team Knock ‘A’ Team out of Cup

The 1983/84 season will be best remembered for the quarter-final cup tie between Athletic ‘A’ & ‘B’ teams with the ‘B’ team emerging victorious after a replay thanks to a Vince Casey goal. A good win for the ‘boys’ but not such a good result for the club! The ‘Sossy’ managed ‘B’ team suffered a heavy defeat in the semi-final as they failed to raise their game against a strong Tralee Dynamos team.

The Growth of Schoolboy Soccer

With the Kerry soccer league now 10 years old many clubs were beginning to realise the importance of promoting underage soccer. Athletic’s underage 7-a-side tournament held in the ‘Áras’ pitch was nurturing a great interest in the game locally while Castleisland man George Callaghan was to the fore in promoting school boys soccer in the county. Vince Casey and Tom ‘Sossy’ Mullins co-managed Athletic’s first schoolboy team in the early ‘80s. The Kerry youth league was also proving to be a very popular competition and contributing to an improvement in the standards of the game in the county.

Needless to say Athletic didn’t win the match but scored on 3 occasions.
